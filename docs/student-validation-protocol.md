# Kelus student validation protocol

## Claim under test

Kelus helps a student prepare more effectively by turning their own course material into a source-grounded route, measuring retrieval, and changing the next route when evidence changes.

This protocol does not assume that the claim is true. It creates comparable evidence for deciding whether it is true.

## Pilot

- Recruit 5-10 students with a real exam 7-30 days away.
- Include at least three subjects and a mixture of text PDFs and scans.
- Ask each student to use Kelus for three sessions of 20-45 minutes.
- Do not coach them through the interface after the opening task prompt.

## Opening task

> Add the material you would normally use for your next exam, tell Kelus when the exam is, and complete today's route. Think aloud when anything is unclear or does not match your course.

## Evidence to record

For each session, record only observable or consented evidence:

- setup completion and time to first route;
- extraction corrections: renamed, removed, or missing concepts;
- citation accuracy for the page or slide shown in the session;
- evaluator disagreements, including the student's answer and expected source claim;
- whether a reroute occurred and whether the student understood why;
- session completion, abandoned step, and stated reason;
- a short pre-session and delayed post-session retrieval check using the same concept but a different prompt.

## Success thresholds for the pilot

- At least 80% reach a cited learning session without help.
- At least 90% of sampled citations point to the claimed source passage.
- Fewer than 15% of answer evaluations are judged clearly wrong by a human reviewer.
- At least 70% can explain why Kelus selected the next activity.
- Delayed retrieval is directionally better after the route for most participants; report the raw sample rather than claiming statistical significance.

## Stop conditions

Pause expansion if any of these occur repeatedly:

- the wrong concept is extracted or cited;
- the evaluator rewards a materially false answer;
- removal leaves a deleted source in a later session;
- a reroute is presented as certain when evidence is weak;
- a student cannot recover from OCR or sync failure.

## Reporting

Report participant count, subjects, completion rates, correction rates, citation accuracy, evaluator disagreements, and raw retrieval changes. Do not publish testimonials, learning gains, or efficacy claims without explicit participant consent and enough evidence.
